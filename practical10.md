# Practical 10: Case Study of Data Privacy

## Aim

To analyze a real-world data privacy case study, understand the privacy risks involved and identify lessons that can be used to improve data protection.

## Case Study: Facebook–Cambridge Analytica

### Introduction

The Facebook–Cambridge Analytica incident is a well-known example of privacy concerns involving social-media data and third-party applications.

### Background

A third-party application developed by Aleksandr Kogan collected information from Facebook users. At the time, the application's permissions also allowed information relating to some users' Facebook friends to be collected.

The information was subsequently provided to Cambridge Analytica.

---

## Privacy Issues Identified

### 1. Third-Party Data Access

The case demonstrated the privacy risks that can occur when third-party applications are given access to user information.

### 2. Lack of User Awareness

Many users did not have a clear understanding of how their information could be collected and used through the application.

### 3. Data Sharing

The case raised concerns about how information collected through one service could subsequently be provided to another organization.

### 4. User Control

The incident highlighted the importance of giving users meaningful control and clear information about how their data is accessed.

### 5. Data Governance

Organizations need proper procedures for controlling, monitoring and auditing access to personal information.

---

## Effects of the Incident

The incident resulted in widespread discussion about:

- Social-media privacy.
- Third-party applications.
- Data sharing.
- User consent.
- Personal information protection.
- Corporate responsibility for user data.

It also increased public and regulatory attention toward how large online platforms handle personal information.

---

## Lessons Learned

### Lesson 1: Limit Third-Party Access

Applications should receive only the permissions that are actually required.

### Lesson 2: Improve Transparency

Users should be clearly informed about what information is being collected and how it will be used.

### Lesson 3: Monitor Data Sharing

Organizations should regularly review how personal information is being accessed and shared.

### Lesson 4: Strong Access Controls

Access to personal information should be limited to authorized applications and people.

### Lesson 5: Regular Auditing

Third-party applications and data-sharing practices should be regularly reviewed.

### Lesson 6: Privacy by Design

Privacy should be considered while designing a system instead of being added only after a problem occurs.

---

## Conclusion

The Facebook–Cambridge Analytica case demonstrates how third-party access to personal information can create significant privacy risks. It shows the importance of transparency, user consent, access control, data governance and regular auditing when handling personal data.

## Result

The case study was successfully analyzed and the major privacy risks, consequences and preventive measures were identified.
